# Story Bible — The Forgotten Clockwork

## Premise
A courier invites the Dragonborn to Veyrhold, a luminous Dwemer-era underground wonder. Mechanical citizens greet them warmly. Their routines repeat because the city's hospitality network is deliberately rehearsing a perfect guest experience. Caldris Veyr, a once-Dwemer inventor now distributed through the machinery, seeks to transfer his consciousness into the Dragonborn's body and send the mechanical Echo back to Skyrim in their place.

## Locked choices
- Opening: voluntary welcoming community, initially free to leave.
- First unsettling event: repeated hospitality day, not a true time loop.
- Morrow: damaged performer with memories belonging to others; rescue possible, not required for progress.
- City: living mechanical civilization with reactive doors, patrols, districts, and persistent citizens.
- Animatronics: Gen I brass founders; Gen II wood/brass theatrical performers; Gen III refined successors.
- Echo: unfinished, unstable, self-aware, treated by Caldris as defective.
- The people before: some souls trapped, some converted, others fragmented. No restoration to lost original bodies.
- Horror: psychological escalation, Midnight Procession, autonomous named hunters, captures of the valuable Dragonborn.
- Gameplay: adaptive encounter routes; cinematic scenes brief and mostly preserve control.
- Moral center: identity versus control.
- Endings: destroy city, liberate its people, or command them and risk becoming Caldris.

## World and tone
Warm, cheerful, believable city at opening. Subtle repeated jokes, routines and gestures imply a script. Morrow reveals the trap; sealed gates expose that the Dragonborn is property. Mechanical infrastructure becomes a hostile part of the city. The inventor is heard through letters and speakers before his throne-machine is revealed.

## Postgame
Each ending changes citizen schedules, faction states and workshop access. Do not damage unrelated Skyrim quest state or follower inventories. An external return route remains available.

## Canon constraints
Original story inspired by Elder Scrolls concepts; do not claim the Dwemer body-transfer techniques are established canon. Define their limits through collectible records and dialogue. All characters and original art must remain separately licensed for distribution.
